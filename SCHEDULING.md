# Calendar and progress policy

Habit completion dates are `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar values in the user's chosen IANA timezone. They are not timestamps and are not rewritten when the timezone setting changes. Event audit times and task completion times use timezone-aware timestamps.

Fixed schedules are `daily`, `weekdays`, `weekends`, and `custom:XXXXXXX` (Monday through Sunday bits). An eligible fixed occurrence is within the habit's start/end dates, on a selected weekday, and not in the future. A rest day, a pre-start day, and a future day are outside the denominator. Archived habits stay in storage but are omitted from current analytics. A zero-denominator rate is shown as no eligible occurrences rather than 0% adherence.

`weekly:N` is a flexible quota. Any date in the habit's active range can record an eligible completion; no individual calendar day is required. A full calendar week starts on the user's configured week-start day for Today quota display. Weekly attainment is `completed through the cutoff >= min(N, active dates in the full week)`. Completions above the quota remain recorded. Weeks can cross month and year boundaries. Today reads the complete week, including dates from adjacent months.

Calendar-month percentages, daily charts, weekly bars, and the top-habits ranking include **fixed schedules only**. They do not divide a weekly quota across partial month weeks. The monthly tracker shows a flexible habit's weekly count for the displayed cutoff week and its separate raw count of checks within that month. The optional activity streak means consecutive calendar days with at least one eligible completion; it is not an all-habits streak.

Time blocks use local wall-clock start and end times and the user's current timezone. Their weekdays use a Monday-first mask. Each series has a stable block ID and effective-dated rule revisions. Editing details, pausing, archiving, and resuming require an effective date of today or later. The latest revision on or before an occurrence's original date defines its title, time, weekday mask, and active state. A change to today's already-started session is rejected; choose a later effective date. Paused and archived revisions suppress future planning only. Earlier planned and recorded occurrences stay visible. Repeated changes on one effective date replace that date's revision; later revisions still take effect on their dates. Existing per-date exceptions remain keyed to the stable block ID and original occurrence date, even when moved to another displayed date.

A per-date exception records a skip, move, start, completion, or restored planned state without changing later occurrences. Skipped sessions remain visible on Week with their original date, title, time, and reason. Restore clears the skip and any move override, checks current collisions, and leaves one planned occurrence. Repeated restores are rejected. A historical skipped session may be restored after a later series pause or archive. Completed sessions are counted only after their effective scheduled end, started sessions only after their start, using the user's IANA timezone; nonexistent and ambiguous local times are rejected. Backfills after those times are allowed. Fixed commitments cannot be skipped or moved through occurrence controls, and a flexible overlap option never permits a fixed-commitment collision. Touching time boundaries are allowed. Recorded tasks and block sessions are separate output counts; a linked task is counted once in the task count. Weekly review counts skipped occurrences once in planned totals and separately as skipped, never as completed.

Migration `0007` copies each legacy block's current rule into an initial revision. For a legacy paused or archived block, the migration places that status at the migration date and treats earlier dates as active. Earlier edits and their original values cannot be reconstructed from legacy rows; recorded exceptions survive with their existing identities. For users with historical timezone changes, old wall-clock times remain interpreted in the current timezone because the existing data model does not store a timezone per revision.

Limitations: the current habit table stores the latest schedule and start/end range, not schedule-version history. Editing a habit schedule can therefore recalculate historical adherence under the new rule. Block times are local wall-clock values; a future timezone change affects their displayed local interpretation. The planner rejects daylight-saving gaps and repeated times when recording starts or completions; it does not auto-adjust them.

## P1 series editing

The Week editor exposes Monday–Sunday checkboxes for a recurring series. A future edit can change title, area, wall-clock times, weekdays, end date, linked task/goal/project, and fixed status. Revision `0008` stores those fields with the effective date, so earlier occurrences use their earlier metadata. Existing recorded exceptions keep their block ID and original date, even if a later weekday or end-date change removes that date from the current rule. A one-off move or time change is still an exception on that occurrence; it does not modify the series.

The server rejects edits before today, changes to a session that has already begun today, invalid or foreign links, and fixed commitment edits without confirmation. Conflicts with fixed blocks cannot be overridden; flexible conflicts require explicit allowance. Pausing, archiving, and resuming a series are effective-dated and keep historical reporting. Calendar-day priorities follow the user's configured timezone; reduced and minimum modes emphasize fewer slots without changing stored assignments.

The series editor's **Check conflicts** control previews a change without saving it and preserves the draft fields. Saving checks again. Create, edit, status, and occurrence mutations take a per-user transaction advisory lock before checking collisions and writing, so simultaneous changes from separate tabs are serialized. A preview can become stale if another tab changes the schedule afterward; Save rechecks under the lock.

## P2 weekly planning and recovery

Week totals use effective occurrences after dated revisions and per-date exceptions. An occurrence belongs to its displayed date, including when moved across a week, month, or year boundary; its original date remains its stable identity. Fixed and flexible scheduled minutes are separate. Flexible time also includes estimates for scheduled tasks without a block in the displayed week. A task linked to a displayed block is not counted twice. Completed sessions remain in scheduled totals and increment the completed count. Only explicitly logged session or task actual minutes enter actual work; for a linked task, a session actual log takes precedence over its task actual log. When none exist, the UI says **Not recorded**. Skipped and excused sessions remain visible but contribute no planned minutes or completions. The daily overload flag compares flexible minutes alone with the user's configured capacity; no capacity means no overload judgment. Blocks cannot cross midnight in this model.

Quick Adjust changes only one flexible occurrence. Move, time, and duration overrides retain the series rule and original occurrence date. Restore clears those overrides after another collision check. Fixed commitments cannot be changed through these controls. A flexible collision needs explicit allowance; a fixed collision is rejected. Each schedule write rechecks under the per-user transaction lock. A conflict preview is advisory because the schedule may change before Save.

The optional fifth gym visit is its own template-keyed flexible series with effective-dated enable, disable, day, time, and activity-type changes. The template's four main sessions remain separate. Light, technique, and mobility visits are distinguishable by title; a disabled visit is rest, not a failed completion. A strict rolling two-training-days-then-rest rule is not implemented, so the user chooses a weekly pattern compatible with recovery.

Time off is an explicit user-selected date, optionally a local time range. Preview lists intersecting fixed commitments and flexible sessions. Saving can excuse only selected flexible sessions; it never changes fixed commitments, habits, or tasks. Excusal is a per-date exception linked to the time-off record, not completion. Cancel restores its linked excusals after checking conflicts. A manually restored occurrence remains independent of the time-off record. Time-off records are retained with cancelled status and private notes; planning the same date again reactivates the retained record. Review reports excused sessions separately; existing habit adherence denominators are unchanged, and a time-off day does not automatically exempt habits.

Reduced and Minimum modes affect visibility only. The stored priority ranks and schedule records remain unchanged, and the full plan is accessible in a disclosure. A minimum action has its own done flag on the day plan; it never writes a full habit entry. Returning to Normal restores the original view.
